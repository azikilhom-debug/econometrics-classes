REAL ECONOMIC QUESTIONLecture 2 · 03/57

If we increase the price by 10%, what happens to sales?

PRICE +10% ↓ SALES = ?
Economic theory shows the expected direction. Econometrics tries to estimate the magnitude of the relationship using data.
How much will sales fall?
Is the effect the same for every product?
What if income changes?
What if a competitor cuts its price?
A precise economic decision requires quantitative evidence, not theory alone.

THE IDEA OF A MODELLecture 2 · 06/57

A model is not an exact copy of reality

REAL ECONOMY — many factors, complex relationships, random events.
SIMPLIFICATION → MODEL
MODEL — key variables, explicit relationships, testable hypotheses.

A good model does not include everything; it captures what is necessary for the question being studied.
MOVING TO A MODELLecture 2 · 07/57

Theory → mathematics → econometrics

Qᵈ = f(P, Y, Pₛ, P꜀, …)
Qᵈᵢ = β₀ + β₁Pᵢ + β₂Yᵢ + uᵢ
1. ECONOMIC THEORY — Other things equal, a higher price is associated with lower demand.
2. MATHEMATICAL REPRESENTATION
3. ECONOMETRIC MODEL
Econometrics allows us to quantify a theoretical relationship using observed data.
CORE DEFINITIONLecture 2 · 08/57

What is an econometric model?

An econometric model expresses theoretical relationships among economic phenomena in mathematical form using statistical data and allows their quantitative relationships to be estimated.
ECONOMIC THEORY + MATHEMATICAL REPRESENTATION + DATA + STATISTICAL METHOD = ECONOMETRIC MODEL
CORE FORMULALecture 2 · 09/57

The basic anatomy of a model

Yᵢ = β₀ + β₁Xᵢ + uᵢ
Yᵢ — dependent variable: the outcome we want to explain.
Xᵢ — explanatory variable: a factor related to the outcome.
β₀ — intercept: the starting level of the model.
β₁ — parameter: the quantitative relationship between X and Y.
uᵢ — random error term: factors omitted from the model.
ECONOMIC INTERPRETATIONLecture 2 · 10/57

A formula is not just a set of symbols

Salesᵢ = β₀ + β₁Advertisingᵢ + uᵢ
Ŷ = 50 + 3X
Sales — dependent variable, the outcome.
Advertising — explanatory variable, the factor being studied.
Interpretation: when X is one unit higher, the model predicts Y to be 3 units higher on average.
Warning: this result by itself does not mean that “X caused Y.”
THE u TERMLecture 2 · 11/57

Why does an econometric model contain a random error term?

Salesᵢ = β₀ + β₁Advertisingᵢ + uᵢ
Product quality
Season
Competitor activity
Consumer preferences
Economic conditions
Weather
Measurement error
Other factors
u — the random error term. In the real economy, Y is usually not fully explained by a single X.
UNCERTAINTYLecture 2 · 12/57

Economic relationships are usually not exact mechanical equations

DETERMINISTIC — Y = 2X. Once X is known, Y is fully determined.
STOCHASTIC — Y = β₀ + β₁X + u. Even when X is known, Y may differ because of other factors.
Econometric models account for economic uncertainty through a random component.
COMPARISONLecture 2 · 13/57

Mathematical model vs econometric model

MATHEMATICAL MODEL — C = a + bY; a theoretical relationship; parameters may remain theoretical symbols; a random component is not required.
ECONOMETRIC MODEL — Cᵢ = β₀ + β₁Yᵢ + uᵢ; uses statistical data; parameters are estimated; a random component is present; hypotheses are tested empirically.
An econometric model connects theory with data.
OBJECTIVESLecture 2 · 14/57

Three main tasks of an econometric model

01 EXPLANATION — Why are some houses more expensive than others?
02 FORECASTING — How much could sales be next quarter?
03 DECISION AND POLICY ANALYSIS — If the interest rate changes, how might economic indicators respond?
The same model is not necessarily equally suitable for all three objectives.
PREDICTION AND EXPLANATIONLecture 2 · 15/57

Are prediction and explanation the same task?

PREDICTION — “Which customer is likely to cancel a subscription?” Goal: the most accurate prediction.
ECONOMIC EXPLANATION — “Which factors are associated with the probability of cancellation?” Goal: understand the economic nature of the relationship.
High predictive accuracy does not always mean a strong economic explanation.
METHODOLOGICAL WARNINGLecture 2 · 16/57

Moving together does not mean causal effect

TEMPERATURE ↑ → ICE-CREAM SALES ↑ and DROWNING INCIDENTS ↑
CORRELATION ≠ CAUSAL EFFECT
Ignoring a third factor can produce a wrong conclusion. Ice-cream sales and drowning may both rise because higher temperature affects both.

Advertising ↑ and Sales ↑

Before holidays

MODEL ELEMENTSLecture 2 · 18/57

Definition: the dependent variable is the outcome explained or predicted by the model.
Y = wage

MODEL ELEMENTSLecture 2 · 19/57

Explanatory variables: what is Y related to?

Wageᵢ = β₀ + β₁Educationᵢ + β₂Experienceᵢ + uᵢ
Wage — dependent variable
Education — explanatory variable
Experience — explanatory variable
Explanatory variables help explain economic differences in Y.
MODEL ELEMENTSLecture 2 · 20/57

Why include control variables?

Wageᵢ = β₀ + β₁Educationᵢ + uᵢ
Wageᵢ = β₀ + β₁Educationᵢ + β₂Experienceᵢ + uᵢ
Research question: What is the relationship between education and wages?
Definition: a control variable is an additional factor included to analyse the main X–Y relationship more accurately.
Warning: control variables should be chosen using theory and the research design.
DUMMY VARIABLELecture 2 · 21/57

How do we put qualitative categories into a model?

Definition: a dummy variable is a categorical indicator that usually takes values 0 and 1.
Onlineᵢ: 1 if the purchase was made online, 0 otherwise.
Crisisₜ: 1 during a crisis period, 0 otherwise.
Dummy variables allow qualitative groups or periods to enter a model.
LAGGED VARIABLELecture 2 · 22/57

Can today’s outcome depend on yesterday’s state?

Inflationₜ = β₀ + β₁Inflationₜ₋₁ + uₜ
Inflationₜ — inflation in the current period.
Inflationₜ₋₁ — inflation in the previous period.
Inflation may show inertia.
Consumption habits can persist over time.
Investment decisions may have delayed effects.
PARAMETERSLecture 2 · 23/57

What do β₀ and β₁ mean economically?

Yᵢ = β₀ + β₁Xᵢ + uᵢ
β₀ — the starting level of the model when X = 0.
β₁ — how Y differs on average when X changes by one unit, within the conditions of the model.
The precise interpretation of β₁ depends on the functional form of the model and measurement units.
PRACTICAL READINGLecture 2 · 24/57

Do not just look at a model — read it economically

Rentᵢ = β₀ + β₁Areaᵢ + β₂Centralᵢ + β₃Renovatedᵢ + uᵢ
Rent — dependent variable.
Floor area — apartment size.
Central — dummy for a central location.
Renovated — dummy for renovation status.
u — other unobserved factors.
Class questions: 1) Which is Y? 2) Which are X? 3) Which are dummies? 4) What may be inside u? 5) What does β₁ mean?
MODEL CLASSIFICATIONLecture 2 · 25/57

Main types of econometric models

DATA STRUCTURE — cross-section, time series, panel.
FUNCTIONAL FORM — linear, nonlinear, logarithmic.
TIME DYNAMICS — static, dynamic.
The model type should match the research question and the structure of the data.
DATA TYPELecture 2 · 26/57

Cross-sectional data: many units, one period

Priceᵢ = β₀ + β₁Areaᵢ + β₂Locationᵢ + β₃Ageᵢ + uᵢ
Example: 500 apartments in Tashkent in 2026.
Question: Why do apartment prices differ within the same period?
Apartment 1 … Apartment 500
Cross-sectional data are observations collected for many units at one point or period in time.
DATA TYPELecture 2 · 27/57

Time series: one process changing over time

Inflationₜ = f(ExchangeRateₜ, InterestRateₜ, …)
2017 → 2018 → 2019 → … → 2026
Definition: time-series data are sequential observations of economic indicators over time.
Examples: inflation, exchange rate, GDP, interest rate, stock price.
DATA TYPELecture 2 · 28/57

Panel data: unit + time

Profitᵢₜ = β₀ + β₁Investmentᵢₜ + uᵢₜ
Example: 100 companies × 10 years.
2017 … 2026; Company A … Company 100.
i = unit, t = time. Panel data allow us to study differences across both units and time.
FUNCTIONAL FORMLecture 2 · 29/57

The core idea of a linear model

Y = β₀ + β₁X + u
Interpretation: in a linear model, the relationship between X and Y is linear in the parameters. A one-unit change in X is associated with an expected change in Y captured by β₁.
The linear model is one of the main starting points in econometric analysis.
NONLINEAR RELATIONSHIPLecture 2 · 30/57

Does the same additional spending always produce the same result?

ADVERTISING EXPENDITURE → SALES
At low spending levels, additional advertising may have a large effect.
At high spending levels, the marginal benefit may fall as the market becomes saturated.
The shape of an economic relationship should be checked using theory and data.
LOGARITHMIC MODELLecture 2 · 31/57

Using logarithms to analyse percentage changes

ln(Yᵢ) = β₀ + β₁ln(Xᵢ) + uᵢ
LEVEL → LOG → % INTERPRETATION
Core idea: in a log–log model, β₁ is often interpreted as an elasticity.
Simple interpretation: a 1% change in X is associated with an approximately β₁% change in Y on average.
This will be studied in more detail in later classes.
STATIC AND DYNAMIC MODELSLecture 2 · 32/57

The role of time in economics

Consumptionₜ = β₀ + β₁Incomeₜ + uₜ
Consumptionₜ = β₀ + β₁Incomeₜ + β₂Consumptionₜ₋₁ + uₜ
STATIC MODEL — current consumption is related to current income.
DYNAMIC MODEL — current consumption is related to current income and previous-period consumption.
A dynamic model captures persistence in economic processes over time.
REAL ECONOMETRIC RESEARCHLecture 2 · 33/57

How did Uber study the demand response to price changes?

Research idea: Cohen, Hahn, Hall, Levitt and Metcalfe used price changes generated by Uber’s surge-pricing algorithm to estimate demand elasticity.
≈ 50 million individual observations
Method: regression discontinuity design
PRICE → RIDE REQUESTS → DEMAND ELASTICITY → BUSINESS DECISION
Big data alone are not enough. The research design must also be credible.
Source: Cohen, Hahn, Hall, Levitt & Metcalfe, NBER Working Paper No. 22627, 2016.
CASE INTERPRETATIONLecture 2 · 34/57

Fifty million observations do not automatically solve the problem

DATA — many observations ≠ automatic causal evidence.
IDENTIFICATION — how do we separate price changes from other factors?
DECISION — demand elasticity, pricing, and consumer surplus.
Econometrics = question + economic logic + data + the right method.
Source: Cohen, Hahn, Hall, Levitt & Metcalfe, NBER Working Paper No. 22627, 2016.

MODELLING STAGESLecture 2 · 40/57

Stage 1. Ask a precise research question

Poor question: “We will study education.”
Better question: “How is an additional year of education associated with wage levels?”
Education → Wage ?
Hypothesis: people with more education are expected to have higher wages on average.
MODELLING STAGESLecture 2 · 41/57

Stage 2. Theory guides the model

Qᵈ = f(P, Y, Pₛ, P꜀, T, …)
P — product price
Y — consumer income
Pₛ — price of a substitute
P꜀ — price of a complement
T — tastes and preferences
Theory helps identify important variables, the expected direction of relationships, and the economic logic of the model.
OPERATIONALISATIONLecture 2 · 42/57

Stage 3. Turn a theoretical concept into a measurable variable

How can “housing quality” be measured? floor area (m²), number of rooms, building age (years), renovated (1/0), distance to the centre (km).
How can “education” be measured? years of schooling, degree level, test score.
In an econometric model, a theoretical concept must be converted into measurable data.

Stage 4. Where do the data come from and how were they collected?

01 Is the source reliable?
02 Is the number of observations sufficient?
03 What period does it cover?
04 What is the data frequency?
05 Are there missing values?
06 Are nominal and real measures distinguished?

MODEL QUALITY CANNOT EXCEED DATA QUALITY.
DATA ERRORLecture 2 · 44/57

“Garbage in → Garbage out”

DATA ERROR — Salary: 5,000,000; 6,500,000; 70,000,000; 6,200,000. Is 70,000,000 a real value or an entry error?
Before estimation: descriptive statistics, missing values, outliers, coding, unit consistency, date consistency.
We must understand and check the data before building a model.
MODEL SPECIFICATIONLecture 2 · 45/57

Stage 5. Turn an economic idea into an explicit equation

Qᵢ = β₀ + β₁Priceᵢ + β₂Incomeᵢ + uᵢ
Theory: Demand = f(Price, Income)
Which Y? Which X? What functional form? Which controls? Do we need a lag? What time period? Which sample?
Definition: model specification is the process of explicitly defining the econometric form of an economic relationship.
SPECIFICATION CHOICELecture 2 · 46/57

Should we include as many variables as possible?

Model A: Sales = β₀ + β₁Advertising + u
Model B: Sales = β₀ + β₁Advertising + β₂Price + β₃Season + β₄Temperature + … + u
Is Model B automatically better? NO.
A variable should enter a model because of economic theory, the research question, and statistical justification.
Complexity by itself is not a sign of quality.
ESTIMATIONLecture 2 · 47/57

Stage 6. Estimate unknown β parameters using data

Population model: Yᵢ = β₀ + β₁Xᵢ + uᵢ
After estimation: Ŷᵢ = β̂₀ + β̂₁Xᵢ
β₀ and β₁ are unknown theoretical parameters.
β̂₀ and β̂₁ are parameter estimates obtained from sample data.
Next topic: ordinary least squares (OLS) and coefficient estimation.
MODEL CHECKINGLecture 2 · 48/57

Stage 7. A regression result is not the end of the research

ECONOMIC LOGIC — Does the coefficient sign agree with theory?
STATISTICAL RELIABILITY — Could the result be explained by random variation?
MODEL DIAGNOSTICS — How well are the model’s statistical assumptions satisfied?
ROBUSTNESS — Does the result survive other reasonable specifications?
An econometric result should not be used for decisions without being checked.
ECONOMIC INTERPRETATIONLecture 2 · 49/57

Stage 8. Turn a number into economic meaning

Ŝales = 120 − 4.2Price + 2.7Advertising
WRONG: “β₁ = −4.2.”
BETTER: holding the other included factors constant, a one-unit higher price is associated with sales that are 4.2 units lower on average.
The value of an econometric result depends on correct economic interpretation.
DIFFERENT KINDS OF SIGNIFICANCELecture 2 · 50/57

Statistically significant — economically important too?

β̂ = 0.00001
STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE — helps assess whether a result could be explained by random error.
ECONOMIC IMPORTANCE — asks whether the size of the effect or relationship is large and meaningful enough for a real decision.
“Statistically significant” and “economically important” are not the same concept.
FORECASTLecture 2 · 51/57

Stage 9. Estimate a future value using the model

Salesₜ = f(Priceₜ, Advertisingₜ, Incomeₜ, …)
Priceₜ₊₁ ↓
Advertisingₜ₊₁ ↓
Incomeₜ₊₁ → Predicted Salesₜ₊₁
A forecast is not the exact future value; it is an estimate based on a model, data and assumptions.

CASE INTERPRETATIONLecture 2 · 56/57

How should we state the result?

Illustrative result: β̂₁ = 350.
WRONG: “Delivery increases sales by 350 units.”
BETTER: controlling for the other included factors, sales were on average 350 units higher on days when delivery was available.
Delivery may have been switched on specifically on high-demand days; it may have started together with promotions; seasonal factors may exist.
Correct econometric interpretation begins with the coefficient, but it does not end with the coefficient.
THE CORE LOGIC OF ECONOMETRIC THINKINGLecture 2 · 57/57

From question to decision

QUESTION → THEORY → VARIABLES → DATA → MODEL → ESTIMATION → TESTING → ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION → FORECAST / DECISION
The goal of econometrics is not to find an equation.
The goal is to search for a credible, data-based answer to an economic question.
Model = simplified representation of the real economy; econometric models contain randomness; defining Y and X correctly matters; correlation is not causation; a result without economic interpretation is not enough.
Next step: estimating a regression model and interpreting coefficients.
