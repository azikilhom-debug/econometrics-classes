KEY DISTINCTIONLecture 1 · 08/48

The difference between opinion and evidence

OPINION — “Advertising increases sales.” This is a logical hypothesis or theoretical view. It requires observations and data to be tested.
EVIDENCE — A systematic analysis of real data on advertising and sales. Evidence can support, reject, or refine an opinion.
OPINION ≠ EVIDENCE

CORE IDEALecture 1 · 13/48

A simple way to understand econometrics

Economics asks a question.
Econometrics searches for an answer using data.
Economic idea — Advertising increases sales.
Econometric question — By how much does advertising change sales?
An idea asks “what might happen?”; an econometric question asks “how can we test this with data?”

THREE PILLARS: 1Lecture 1 · 15/48

What does economics give us?

Economic theory explains how phenomena may be related and creates theoretical propositions that should be tested.
Simple example — Other things equal, when a product’s price rises, demand may fall. This is a testable economic hypothesis.
Economics guides us on what type of relationship we should expect.
THREE PILLARS: 2Lecture 1 · 16/48

What does mathematics give us?

Demand = f(Price)
This notation says that quantity demanded may be related to price.
Why do we need it? Mathematics helps express an economic idea in a short, precise and structured form. At this point we are not calculating anything; we are only defining the relationship.
We introduce the formula after the concept: meaning first, notation second.
THREE PILLARS: 3Lecture 1 · 17/48

What does statistics give us?

Organises — structures data as tables and indicators.
Summarises — turns many observations into understandable results.
Shows differences — helps us see averages and dispersion.
Studies relationships — helps us examine how indicators move together.
Econometrics uses these statistical tools to answer economic questions.

Y AND XLecture 1 · 25/48

The simplest relationship

Y = f(X)
This notation means that the value of Y may be related to X.
Example 1 — Sales = f(Advertising): sales may be related to advertising expenditure.
Example 2 — Consumption = f(Income): consumption may be related to income.
These expressions do not yet prove a causal effect; they describe the relationship to be studied.

DATALecture 1 · 28/48

Why do we need data?

Without data — we only have a hypothesis and cannot clearly separate which factors matter and by how much.
With data — we can test a hypothesis using observations, compare outcomes, and draw evidence-based conclusions.
Econometrics tests theoretical ideas using real observations.
Data are the observations and measurements used for analysis.

OBJECTIVESLecture 1 · 33/48

Objective 2: quantify the effect

Simple statement — “Advertising increases sales.” This gives a direction but not a magnitude.
Econometric question — “If advertising expenditure rises by 1 million UZS, by how much does sales change on average?”
Econometrics aims not only to identify whether a relationship exists, but also, where possible, to estimate its magnitude.

SUMMARYLecture 1 · 38/48

Main tasks of econometrics

Identify — define the problem and relevant factors.
Measure — estimate the magnitude of relationships.
Test — confront theoretical ideas with data.
Forecast — provide an evidence-based estimate for the future.
Decide — make choices using the results.
These tasks are not separate; they work as stages of one research process.

IMPORTANT WARNINGLecture 1 · 45/48

Association is not always causation

Observed in summer — ice-cream sales rise and drowning incidents also rise.
Big question — Did ice cream cause drowning?
Two events can move together and therefore be associated, but that does not mean one causes the other.
ASSOCIATION AND CAUSALITYLecture 1 · 46/48

A third factor

Air temperature ↑
Outcome 1 — In hot weather, people consume more ice cream.
Outcome 2 — In hot weather, more people go swimming.
The existence of an association does not automatically imply a causal effect.
